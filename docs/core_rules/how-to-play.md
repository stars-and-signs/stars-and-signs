# How to Play

## About the Setting

Stars & Signs is assumed to take place in a modern setting inspired by the "magical girl" genre of anime with influences from cosmic and weird horror. Protagonists are young people who must balance magical conflicts against horrors beyond comprehension with their mundane lives fraught with melodrama.

A typical gameplay scenario involves investigation and interactions with casts of interesting characters followed by magical conflicts in surreal dream worlds.

## Gameplay Summary

Stars & Signs (S&S) is a pen-and-paper based roleplaying game for groups of four to six. One player takes on the role of referee, who serves as the arbiter of the rules, narrator, designer of scenarios, and portrays most of the characters in the world. The other players have control over a single character, their protagonist, who's choices and actions drive the story.

To play Stars & Signs, protagonists describe their Actions, the referee then determines if a Check is needed to determine the outcome. Checks are made by rolling 2d10 then adding an Ability die and a Skill modifier. The outcome of a Check is determined by comparing the result of this roll, with a 10 or greater being a success. The degree of success is determined by how much greater than 10 the result is.

When protagonists capabilities are being evaluated without them taking an Action, such as determining if they're able to remember a fact or notice an enemy sneaking up on them, the referee makes a Test. Tests are made by rolling 2d10 and comparing the result against scores that correspond to protagonist Abilities and Skills.

Protagonists have nine Abilities, which represent broad modes of action. Each Ability has three Skills, which are more direct descriptions of action types. Protagonists also have a wide variety of Traits, which are freeform descriptors of their capabilities.
    - **Abilities.** Range in rank from 1 to 10. Higher ranks result in higher-faced Ability dice.
    - **Skills.** Range in rank from 0 to 10. Higher ranks result in higher Skill modifiers. Can be compelled to regain Effort.
    - **Traits.** Can be "invoked" by spending Hope. When invoked, a protagonist gains a Boon for a Check or an Advantage die that can improve a success. Can also be compelled to regain Hope by roleplaying a negative aspect of the Trait.

The difficulty of Checks and Tests may be modified by Boons and Banes. Boons and Banes increase the number of dice of the specified type are rolled, discarding either the lowest or highest dice. Protagonists may increase their odds of success by spending resources called Effort and Hope.

Protagonists may also improve their odds of success using Assets, which can represent physical or abstract tools. Assets allow protagonists to either replace their Ability die with a better die or add a flat bonus to their Checks. Only one Asset can be used at a time.

Whenever a protagonist takes an Action, the referee introduces a Complication. Complications represent the world reacting to the protagonists efforts. When a protagonist fails at an Action or Test, the referee introduces Fallout, which represents a serious consequence corresponding to the narrative.

Protagonists suffer repercussions in the form of gaining Despair, Moods, Corruption, and Wounds.
    - **Despair.** A "Counter" with ten slots. Each slot absorbs 5 points of Despair. Despair is suffered as a common consequence. A protagonist begins to suffer Wounds when their Counter fills. A protagonist with 5 filled slots becomes worse at everything.
    - **Moods.** Used to absorb Despair. These transient states can be cleared with roleplay.
    - **Wounds.** Lasting injuries that are physical, mental, or social in nature. They're more serious than Despair and harder to heal. Gaining too many Wounds results in a protagonist being lost.
    - **Corruption.** A ten slot Counter protagonists fill to use their magical Transformations. Mysterious consequences occur as it fills. A protagonist that fills their meter experiences a catastrophic fall to darkness. Corruption can be reduced through interacting with mundane things the protagonists care about.

Protagonists can perform Transformations by gaining Corruption, which allow them to use free-form magic. While Transformed, a protagonist slowly depletes Energy until they must gain additional Corruption to maintain it.

## Basic Gameplay Principles

* **Round up.** When a calculation results in a non-integer number, unless otherwise specified, round up to the higher nearest integer.
